# Towable RV (Travel Trailer) Recurring Maintenance Tasks & Service Intervals

**Scope:** Recurring maintenance and service tasks for a **towable travel trailer** (bumper-pull or fifth-wheel style running gear, RV house systems). Motorhome-only items (chassis engine/transmission, motive drivetrain) are excluded.

**Date compiled:** 2026-07-23

**Source-priority note:** This document prioritizes sources with **no direct financial incentive** to sell service or parts, in this order: (1) government / safety bodies (NHTSA, PHMSA/DOT, US Fire Administration, NFPA), (2) standards bodies and component **manufacturer owner/service manuals** used for factory-spec intervals only (Dexter axles, Onan/Cummins generators, appliance makers), (3) RV industry association consumer education (Go RVing / RV Industry Association) and owner clubs, (4) independent consumer education. Dealer blogs, mobile-tech "schedule your service" pages, and parts-retailer content are **downweighted** and flagged where they are the only support for a claim. Manufacturer manuals have an indirect incentive (warranty/liability) but are the authoritative source for *factory* intervals, so they are treated as high-authority for spec numbers only.

> **Important modeling note for rv-checklist:** Many of these tasks are **NOT calendar-based**. Several are **mileage-based OR calendar-based, whichever comes first** (wheel bearings, brakes), **event-based** (lug-nut re-torque after any wheel removal; pre-trip checks), or **seasonal** (winterize = fall; de-winterize + sanitize = spring). The current flat 12-month model is wrong for roughly half of these. See the "Notable mismatches vs. a flat 12-month model" section.

## Task-by-task detail

### Running gear & tow

#### 1. Wheel bearing repack / lubricate & inspect
**Interval: every 12 months OR 12,000 miles, whichever comes first.** This is the factory spec from Dexter, the dominant trailer-axle maker: "Bearings should be lubricated every 12 months or 12,000 miles." Dexter also notes the **calendar** side matters even for a trailer that mostly sits, because grease separates/bleeds and leaves upper rollers dry and prone to condensation rust — so the "or 12 months" clause is the binding one for low-mileage trailers.
- Dexter, *How to Manually Repack Bearings on a Trailer*: https://www.dextergroup.com/Resources/Learning-Library/How-To/5545/how-to-manually-repack-bearings-on-a-trailer
- Dexter *Operation • Maintenance • Service Manual* (600–8,000 lb, PDF): https://www.dextergroup.com/user_area/content_media/raw/600-8k_complete_service_manual.pdf (mirror: https://lance-camper.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/docs/manuals/Dexter-axles.pdf)

**Basis:** dual — calendar **and** mileage, whichever first. A flat 12-month model is roughly right on the calendar leg but ignores the 12,000-mile leg for heavy travelers.

#### 2. Trailer electric brakes — inspection & adjustment
**Inspect** magnets, shoes, and drums at each bearing service (**~annually / 12,000 mi**). **Adjustment:** Dexter specifies brakes be adjusted **after the first ~200 miles** of operation (once shoes/drums seat), then **at 3,000-mile intervals** or "as use and performance requires." Non-self-adjusting brakes need periodic manual adjustment; many newer Dexter brakes self-adjust. Magnets and linings are wear items inspected for thickness at each service.
- Dexter service manual (as above): brake adjustment "after the first 200 miles… at 3,000 mile intervals, or as use and performance requires."

**Basis:** mileage-driven (3,000 mi) plus an initial break-in event (200 mi) — **not annual**.

#### 3. Tire inflation pressure
**Check cold inflation pressure before every trip and at least once a month.** NHTSA/TireWise and the US Tire Manufacturers Association (USTMA) recommend monthly cold-pressure checks; RV/trailer tires lose pressure over storage and under-inflation is the leading cause of trailer-tire failure. Always inflate to the trailer's placard / tire sidewall max for load, not the tow vehicle's number.
- NHTSA TireWise — *Tires*: https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/tires

**Basis:** event (pre-trip) + calendar (monthly). More frequent than annual.

#### 4. Tire tread & condition inspection
Inspect for tread depth, cracking, bulges, and uneven wear regularly. Replace at **2/32"** remaining tread (NHTSA minimum). For trailers, sidewall cracking/aging usually condemns the tire before tread wears out.
- NHTSA TireWise: https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/tires

#### 5. Tire age retirement (date code)
**Replace by ~6 years of age regardless of tread, and never exceed ~10 years.** NHTSA has *no federal age mandate* and defers to vehicle/tire-maker guidance, but notes many manufacturers recommend replacement at **6–10 years**. USTMA recommends tires be taken out of service by **10 years** maximum and inspected annually after 5 years. For **trailer (ST) tires specifically**, industry and owner-club guidance is more conservative — often **3–6 years** — because trailer tires sit parked, UV/ozone-exposed, and degrade internally while looking new. Read the **DOT Tire Identification Number**: the last four digits are week+year of manufacture (e.g., "3018" = 30th week of 2018).
- NHTSA TireWise (aging, defers to mfr, DOT TIN): https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/tires
- NTSB Tire Safety Symposium (tire aging research, government): https://www.ntsb.gov/news/events/Documents/2014_Tire_Safety_SYM_Panel_4a_Soodoo.pdf
- *(Trailer-specific 3–6 yr framing seen widely, incl. commercially-incentivized retailer pages such as https://aaatrailer.com/pages/trailer-tire-age-6-year-rule — flagged: seller of trailer tires. Use NHTSA/NTSB for the underlying safety principle.)*

**Basis:** age (date code), not service calendar. Cannot be modeled as a recurring N-month task; it is a one-time-per-tire-set retirement driven by manufacture date.

#### 6. Lug-nut / wheel-nut torque
**Torque to the trailer's spec, then re-torque after the first ~10, 25, and 50 miles of driving, and again after ANY wheel removal.** New/re-mounted wheels settle against the hub and lose clamp load; re-torque after the first ~50 miles is standard across trailer-axle and trailer makers. This is a repeating **event-driven** task tied to wheel service, not the calendar.
- Trailer Safety Week (industry safety coalition), *How to Torque Trailer Lug Nuts*: https://www.trailersafetyweek.com/blog/2025/5/28/how-to-torque-trailer-lug-nuts-a-step-by-step-guide
- Load Trail wheel-torque spec sheet (trailer mfr, PDF): https://loadtrail.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Wheel-Torque-Specs.pdf
- Sure-Trac wheel-torque specs (trailer mfr, PDF): https://sure-trac.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Wheel-Torque-Specs.pdf

**Basis:** **event** (after any wheel-off) + a break-in mileage sequence. Not calendar-based at all.

#### 7. Hitch, coupler & weight-distribution / sway hitch
Inspect and lubricate **before every trip**: grease the hitch ball and coupler socket, verify coupler latch and safety pin, check weight-distribution bars/brackets and sway control for wear and correct adjustment. No fixed calendar interval — it's a pre-departure event check plus periodic re-greasing.
- Escapees/owner and pre-trip guidance; RV-PRO pre-trip checklist (trade magazine): https://rv-pro.com/features/comprehensive-summer-rv-maintenance-pre-trip-checklist/

**Basis:** event (pre-trip).

#### 8. Safety chains
Inspect **before every trip** for stretched/damaged links and worn hooks; confirm they are crossed under the coupler in an X with correct slack and hooks facing inward. Event-driven.
- JACO / RV pre-trip inspection guidance: https://jacosuperiorproducts.com/blogs/news/the-complete-rv-and-travel-trailer-pre-trip-inspection-checklist *(retailer — flagged; principle is standard DOT towing practice)*

**Basis:** event (pre-trip).

#### 9. Breakaway system (switch + battery)
**Test before every trip.** Confirm the breakaway battery holds charge (press the test button / check indicator LED), verify the pull-pin lanyard is intact and not dragging, and that pulling the pin energizes the trailer brakes. The small breakaway battery must be kept charged; a dead breakaway battery = no emergency braking if the trailer separates.
- Keystone RV, *Essential Guide to Testing Your RV Breakaway Switch* (OEM owner education): https://www.keystonerv.com/owners/what-is-a-breakaway-switch
- Curt breakaway kit install/maintenance manual (component mfr, PDF): https://assets.curtmfg.com/masterlibrary/52044/installsheet/52044_INS.pdf

**Basis:** event (pre-trip) + keep-charged.

#### 10. Suspension, shackles, equalizer & wet bolts
Inspect springs, shackles, equalizers, and U-bolts **annually** (or each bearing service) for cracks, wear, and loose fasteners. If the trailer has greasable "wet bolts," grease per the equalizer maker's interval (commonly annually / ~12,000 mi). Dry standard bolts/bushings wear and cause axle misalignment.
- Dexter service manual (suspension inspection): https://www.dextergroup.com/user_area/content_media/raw/600-8k_complete_service_manual.pdf

**Basis:** calendar (annual) / mileage.

### Exterior & structure

#### 11. Roof & exterior sealant / seam inspection
**Inspect all roof and exterior seams, seals, and sealant every ~90 days (3 months).** This is the near-universal RV-manufacturer recommendation (and warranties can be voided if the interval isn't met). Look at roof edges, vents, skylights, antenna bases, window/door frames, and body seams for cracking, gaps, or lifting sealant; touch up lap sealant as needed.
- Venture RV (OEM), *Roof Inspection & Resealing* — states the 90-day inspection: https://www.venture-rv.com/maintenance-tips/roof-inspection-resealing/
- RVing with Mark Polk (independent educator), *How Do I Reseal My RV Roof Seams & Sealants*: https://rvingwithmarkpolk.com/2012/03/18/how-do-i-reseal-my-rv-roof-seams-sealants/

**Basis:** calendar (quarterly) — **4× more frequent than annual.**

#### 12. Full roof reseal / recoat
Reseal/recoat the membrane as condition dictates — typically a **full reseal around every ~10 years** for EPDM/TPO roofs, with lap-sealant touch-ups far more often (found during the 90-day inspections). Condition-driven, not a fixed calendar task.
- Camping World / industry guidance on seams & sealants: https://blog.campingworld.com/learn-to-rv/everything-you-need-to-know-about-rv-seams-sealants/ *(retailer blog — flagged; use for the ~decade ballpark only)*

**Basis:** condition / long-cycle.

#### 13. Slide-out seals (clean & condition)
Clean and treat the wiper/bulb seals with an RV seal conditioner **at least twice a year** (top, bottom, sides, both faces) to keep them supple and watertight. More often in coastal/dusty use.
- Carefree of Colorado (component mfr), *RV Slideout Maintenance*: https://www.carefreeofcolorado.com/blog/rv-slideout-maintenance/
- KOA (owner-facing), *Lubricating and Maintaining Your RV Slide-Outs*: https://koa.com/blog/lubricating-rv-slide-outs/

**Basis:** calendar/seasonal (semi-annual).

#### 14. Slide-out mechanism lubrication
Lubricate rails/gears/rams with a **dry** Teflon or graphite lubricant **about every 3 months** (monthly in salt air or desert dust; minimum twice a year). Avoid oily sprays that attract grit.
- KOA: https://koa.com/blog/lubricating-rv-slide-outs/
- Harvest Hosts, *Basics of RV Slideout Maintenance*: https://www.harvesthosts.com/blog/basics-rv-slideout-maintenance

**Basis:** calendar (quarterly).

#### 15. Awning
Clean the fabric periodically (mildew/dirt) and inspect fabric, arms, and hardware **seasonally**; extend/retract to check operation. No strict manufacturer calendar interval — condition/seasonal.
- Campers and Campfires, *7 Ways to Care for Your Camper Slides and Awnings*: https://campersandcampfires.com/slide-awning-maintenance/ *(independent blog — low authority)*

**Basis:** seasonal / condition.

#### 16. Stabilizer jacks / landing gear
Lubricate scissor-jack screws / landing-gear gearboxes and inspect **seasonally / periodically**; keep threads clean and greased for smooth, quiet operation.
- KOA lubrication guide (covers jacks): https://koa.com/blog/lubricating-rv-slide-outs/

**Basis:** seasonal / periodic.

### Water systems

#### 17. Fresh-water system sanitization
**Sanitize at least twice a year, and specifically at spring de-winterizing, before first use after long storage, and any time the water tastes/smells off.** The bleach method is what RV standards and public-health guidance endorse: NFPA 1192 (Standard on Recreational Vehicles) publishes the sanitizing procedure, and the CDC endorses chlorine (household bleach) disinfection of potable water. Common dose: ~¼ cup (≈2 oz) of unscented 5–6% household bleach per 15 gallons of tank capacity, fill, circulate to all taps, let stand ~4–12 hours, then flush until no chlorine smell.
- CDC, *Making Water Safe in an Emergency* (bleach disinfection of drinking water): https://www.cdc.gov/healthywater/emergency/making-water-safe.html
- EPA, *Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water*: https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water
- NFPA 1192 (Standard on Recreational Vehicles) — publishes the RV fresh-water sanitizing procedure: https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-1192-standard/p1192code
- Camping World procedure (steps/dose): https://blog.campingworld.com/learn-to-rv/sanitize-your-rvs-freshwater-system-in-5-simple-steps/ *(retailer — flagged; dose corroborated by NFPA 1192/CDC principle)*

**Basis:** seasonal (spring) + event (post-storage/off-taste). **Far more frequent and event-driven than annual.**

#### 18. Winterize plumbing
**Every fall, once, in any climate that freezes.** Blow out or fill the water lines, water heater bypass, and traps with **non-toxic RV antifreeze** (never automotive ethylene-glycol). Skipping this risks split pipes, pump, and water heater.
- Go RVing (RV Industry Association consumer site), *Complete Guide to Winterizing Your RV*: https://www.gorving.com/tips-inspiration/expert-advice/go-rvings-complete-guide-winterizing-your-rv

**Basis:** **seasonal (fall)** — an annual event, but tied to season, not an arbitrary month from purchase.

#### 19. De-winterize plumbing
**Every spring, once,** before first use: flush antifreeze out of all lines until it runs clear and odorless, reinstall filters/water-heater drain plug/anode, pressure-test for leaks, then **sanitize** (task #17).
- Carefree of Colorado, *How to Dewinterize Your RV*: https://www.carefreeofcolorado.com/blog/how-to-dewinterize-your-rv/
- Go RVing (RVIA): https://www.gorving.com/tips-inspiration/expert-advice/go-rvings-complete-guide-winterizing-your-rv

**Basis:** **seasonal (spring).**

#### 20. Water-heater flush
Drain and flush the tank of sediment **several times a year with regular use, and at minimum before winterizing.** Sediment reduces capacity and efficiency and accelerates tank/anode corrosion.
- RV Inspection & Care (independent educator), *Your RV Water Heater Anode Rod*: https://rvinspectionandcare.com/water-heater-anode-rod/

**Basis:** calendar / seasonal (multiple times per year).

#### 21. Water-heater anode rod (Suburban / steel tanks only)
**Inspect the anode rod annually** (and drain/flush at the same time); **replace every 1–3 years**, or sooner when ~⅔ or more is consumed. Note: **Atwood/Dometic aluminum-clad tanks have no anode rod** and don't need one — this task only applies to Suburban (and other steel-tank) heaters. Full-timers should check twice a year.
- RV Inspection & Care: https://rvinspectionandcare.com/water-heater-anode-rod/

**Basis:** calendar (annual inspect) + condition (replace 1–3 yr). Model as annual, but only for steel-tank heaters.

#### 22. Water pump inlet screen / inline water filter
Replace disposable inline/carbon filters and clean the pump inlet strainer **about every 3–6 months** (or each season / per manufacturer). Clogged filters starve the pump and can taint water.
- Independent/owner guidance (low authority, no single owning source).

**Basis:** calendar (quarterly–semiannual).

### Safety systems

#### 23. Smoke alarm — test & replace
**Test monthly. Replace the entire alarm every 10 years** (or sooner if it fails a test), including sealed 10-year-battery units. This comes from the US Fire Administration and NFPA 72 and applies to RV smoke alarms too. Replace the battery per the unit (typically annually for replaceable-battery types).
- US Fire Administration (FEMA), *Smoke Alarms*: https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/smoke-alarms/
- NFPA 1192 requires listed smoke, CO, and LP alarms in RVs: https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-1192-standard/p1192code

**Basis:** calendar — test monthly (more frequent than annual); replace on a 10-year cycle (less frequent than annual).

#### 24. Carbon monoxide (CO) alarm — test & replace
**Test monthly. Replace per the printed expiration — typically 5–7 years** (many RV LP/CO combo units are rated 5 years). CO alarms have a finite sensor life regardless of battery.
- US Fire Administration / general fire-service guidance (CO replace 5–7 yr): https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/at-home/carbon-monoxide/
- NFPA 1192 (requires CO alarm in RVs): https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-1192-standard/p1192code

**Basis:** calendar — monthly test; 5–7 year replacement.

#### 25. LP (propane) gas leak detector — test & replace
**Test monthly** (press test button). **Replace every ~5 years** — RV LP detectors are printed with a manufacture date and a 5-year (approx.) expiration because the sensing element degrades. A drained house battery or an expired sensor leaves you unprotected against a propane leak pooling at floor level.
- RV Enthusiast, *Testing and Replacing LP-gas, CO and Smoke Detectors* (magazine, non-retail): https://rventhusiast.com/rvexpert/staying-safe-testing-and-replacing-lp-gas-carbon-monoxide-and-smoke-detectors/
- NFPA 1192 (requires listed LP alarm): https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-1192-standard/p1192code

**Basis:** calendar — monthly test; ~5-year replacement.

#### 26. Fire extinguisher inspection
**Monthly visual inspection** (gauge in green, pin/seal intact, no damage/corrosion, mounted and accessible) and a **thorough annual maintenance check**, per NFPA 10. RVs must carry a listed extinguisher (NFPA 1192 requires a minimum 5-B:C, commonly 10-B:C). Dry-chemical units also need internal exam at 6 years and hydrostatic test at 12 years, but for RV owners the practical recurring tasks are monthly + annual.
- NFPA 10, *Standard for Portable Fire Extinguishers* (monthly inspection / annual maintenance): summarized at MN Dept. of Health (gov): https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/regulation/engineering/docs/lscfireext.pdf
- NFPA 1192 (requires extinguisher in RV): https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-1192-standard/p1192code

**Basis:** calendar — monthly + annual. Monthly is more frequent than annual.

#### 27. Propane system leak / pressure-drop test
Have the **whole LP system pressure-tested (timed manometer drop test) at least annually** by a qualified RV tech (also after any propane work). Between services, do a **smell + soapy-water check on connections before each use** and any time you smell gas. The formal test sets the system to ~8–11" WC and watches for pressure drop over ~3 minutes.
- National RV Training Academy (RV-tech education, not a parts seller), *How to Test for Propane Leaks the Safe and Correct Way*: https://nrvta.com/how-to-test-for-propane-leaks-in-your-rv-the-safe-and-correct-way/

**Basis:** calendar (annual professional test) + event (before-use smell check).

#### 28. Propane regulator replacement
Replace the two-stage/automatic-changeover regulator **roughly every 5–15 years** (manufacturers cite 5–10-year service life; industry rule of thumb caps at ~15 years) or whenever appliances show low/uneven flame, sooting, or the regulator freezes/weeps. Regulators are sealed, non-serviceable units.
- Cavagna/Marshall-type regulator service-life guidance via RVshare, *RV Propane Regulator — When to Replace*: https://rvshare.com/blog/rv-propane-regulator/ *(consumer education; regulator makers publish 5–10 yr)*

**Basis:** calendar / condition (5–15 yr).

#### 29. Propane cylinder (DOT) recertification
**DOT portable cylinders** (the removable 20/30/40 lb bottles on a travel trailer) must be **requalified 12 years from the manufacture date**, then **every 5 years thereafter** if inspected by the external visual method, **or every 12 years** if requalified by volumetric-expansion testing (49 CFR 180.209(e), as amended 2020). The manufacture date is stamped on the collar. **Permanently mounted ASME tanks** (typical on motorhomes, not most trailers) need **no** periodic recertification.
- eCFR, 49 CFR 180.205 — *General requirements for requalification of specification cylinders*: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-180/subpart-C/section-180.205
- eCFR, 49 CFR 180.209 — *Requirements for requalification of specification cylinders* (12-yr/5-yr periods): https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-180/subpart-C/section-180.209
- PHMSA, *Is Your Cylinder Safe to Fill?* (consumer PDF): https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/sites/phmsa.dot.gov/files/2023-08/Is-Your-Cylinder-Safe-to-Fill-PHH50-0201-0323.pdf

**Basis:** calendar (age) — first at 12 years, then 5- or 12-year cycles. Only applies to DOT (removable) cylinders.

### Electrical

#### 30. Battery service (flooded lead-acid)
For flooded lead-acid house/starting batteries: **check electrolyte level monthly** (and after each trip when charging often), topping only with distilled water to ~⅛" below the fill well; clean/anti-corrode terminals; verify state of charge. Sealed AGM/lithium batteries don't need watering but still benefit from periodic charge/terminal checks and correct storage charging.
- RV 101 with Mark Polk (independent educator), *Checking & Adding Water to an RV Battery*: https://rv101withmarkpolk.com/2017/07/23/checking-adding-water-to-an-rv-battery/

**Basis:** calendar (monthly) for flooded cells — more frequent than annual.

#### 31. Converter / charger check
Verify the converter/charger output (~13.6 V float, up to ~14.4 V absorption) with a multimeter **about annually** or if you see lights dimming/flickering, buzzing, or overheating. Keep it ventilated and dust-free.
- National Vehicle, *5 Must-Do RV Electrical System Checks*: https://nationalvehicle.com/5-must-do-rv-electrical-system-checks/ *(dealer-adjacent — flagged; task is routine electrical verification)*

**Basis:** calendar (annual) / condition.

#### 32. GFCI outlet test
**Press Test then Reset on each GFCI monthly** to confirm it trips and resets — this protects the RV's wet-area circuits. GFCI devices can fail silently, so monthly testing is the standard electrical-safety recommendation.
- Standard GFCI-manufacturer / electrical-safety guidance (press-test monthly); corroborated in RV electrical checklists such as https://nationalvehicle.com/5-must-do-rv-electrical-system-checks/

**Basis:** calendar (monthly) — more frequent than annual.

### Appliances

#### 33. Absorption refrigerator (Norcold / Dometic)
**Annual** service: clean the burner/orifice and flue, check for soot, verify flame and ventilation; have safety checks done by an authorized tech. Between services, **inspect the rear vent/coil airflow every 3–6 months** (spiders love the burner tube). Keep it level in storage.
- Norcold owner/service manual (OEM, PDF): https://forestriverinc.com/brochures/0000/0000RefrigeratorNORCOLDN611VN811VManual.pdf

**Basis:** calendar — annual burner service + quarterly vent checks.

#### 34. Furnace (forced-air LP)
**Annual** service before heating season: clean burner and blower, check igniter/sail switch, and clear the exhaust/intake of debris and insect nests. Run it monthly off-season to keep it functional.
- Dometic furnace maintenance guidance (via dealer service center — flagged): https://greatamericanrv.com/blog/understanding-your-dometic-rv-furnace-operation-troubleshooting-and-maintenance/

**Basis:** calendar / seasonal (annual).

#### 35. Roof air conditioner
**Clean or replace the return-air filter monthly during use** — the single most important A/C task. **Annually**, clean the condenser/evaporator coils, check gaskets/bolts (roof mount), and clear the drain/weep holes.
- Dometic A/C maintenance guidance (via dealer service center — flagged): https://greatamericanrv.com/blog/understanding-dometic-rv-air-conditioning-systems-operation-diagnostics-and-maintenance/

**Basis:** calendar — monthly filter (more frequent than annual) + annual coil service.

#### 36. Generator (only if the trailer carries one — e.g., Onan/Cummins)
Most travel trailers don't have an onboard generator, but toy haulers and some larger units do. For Cummins Onan RV gensets:
- **Exercise monthly** for ~1–2 hours under load (≈½ rated load) to keep fuel/oil circulating and windings dry.
- **Oil & filter:** first change at **20 hours or 1 year**, then every **100–150 hours or annually**, whichever first.
- **Air filter:** every **~150 hours or annually** (sooner in dust).
- **Spark arrestor:** clean **annually / 150 hours**.
- **Fuel filter:** every **~500 hours or 2 years**.
- Cummins Onan RV generator maintenance overview (owner-facing, non-retail): https://www.thervgeeks.com/onan-rv-generator-maintenance/
- Consult the specific Onan model *Operator's Manual* for exact hour figures (they vary by model, e.g., QG 2500/4000 vs. QD diesel).

**Basis:** **run-hours OR calendar**, plus a monthly exercise event. Purely hour-driven for heavy users; annual is the fallback minimum.

## Notable mismatches vs. a flat 12-month model

**Clearly MORE frequent than annual (a 12-month interval under-serves these):**
- Tire pressure check — **monthly + pre-trip**
- Roof/exterior sealant & seam inspection — **every 90 days**
- Slide-out mechanism lubrication — **~quarterly**
- Slide-out seal conditioning — **twice a year**
- Water-heater flush — **several times a year**
- Water pump/inline filter — **every 3–6 months**
- Smoke / CO / LP alarm **testing** — **monthly**
- Fire extinguisher visual inspection — **monthly**
- Flooded battery water/charge check — **monthly**
- GFCI test — **monthly**
- A/C return-air filter — **monthly in use**
- Fresh-water sanitizing — **≥ twice a year** (plus event-driven)
- Fridge vent/airflow check — **every 3–6 months**
- Generator exercise — **monthly**

**Clearly LESS frequent than annual (12-month over-serves / wastes effort):**
- Wheel bearing repack — **12 mo / 12,000 mi** (annual is OK as the calendar leg, but low-mileage trailers genuinely hit the time leg, not mileage)
- Full roof reseal — **~10 years**
- Tire age retirement — **~6 years** (10-yr max)
- Smoke alarm **replacement** — **10 years**
- CO alarm **replacement** — **5–7 years**
- LP detector **replacement** — **~5 years**
- Propane regulator — **5–15 years**
- Propane cylinder recertification — **12 years, then 5-yr cycles**
- Water-heater anode rod replacement — **1–3 years**

**Mileage- or run-hour-driven (a calendar model alone is wrong):**
- Wheel bearings — 12,000 **miles** (or 12 months)
- Brake adjustment — first **200 mi**, then every **3,000 mi**
- Generator oil/air/fuel service — **run-hours** (100–500 hr) with an annual fallback

**Event-driven (triggered by an action, not the clock):**
- Lug-nut re-torque — after the first **10/25/50 miles** and after **every wheel removal**
- Hitch/coupler/WDH, safety chains, breakaway test — **before every trip**
- Fresh-water sanitize — after **long storage** or **off taste** (in addition to seasonal)
- Propane smell/soap check — **before each use** and **after any LP work**

**Seasonal (tied to season, not an arbitrary month offset):**
- Winterize — **fall**
- De-winterize + sanitize — **spring**
- Furnace service — **before heating season**; A/C coil service — **before cooling season**

**Modeling recommendation for rv-checklist:** a single "interval in months" field cannot represent tasks 1–2 (mileage-or-calendar), 6 (event + break-in miles), 5/23–25/28–29 (multi-year age-based replacements), 17/27 (seasonal + event), or 36 (run-hours). Consider an interval **basis** enum (calendar / mileage / hours / event / seasonal) plus, for calendar tasks, a real per-task month value instead of a flat 12.
